# Lesson

`oas okf harvest` spawns its harvester three ways, decided by the source instance's custody:

- **repo-resident soul** → attached to the source work tree, one commit on that shared tree;
- **workspace-mode soul** → a worktree of the soul's OWN home repo, commit, push, open a PR,
  never merge;
- **uncommitted local soul** → nothing to commit at all; the edit IS the delivery.

The skill said, unconditionally: you are ATTACHED, finish with one commit on the shared tree.
Two of the three spawned harvesters were therefore reading instructions that did not describe
their situation — and the briefing that DID describe it was the thing they had been told to
follow "per your memory-harvest skill".

**When one skill serves several spawn paths, either enumerate the paths or say plainly that
the briefing is the authority.** I did both: the ground rules now name the briefing as
authoritative for mode, paths and finish, and the finish section lists the three deliveries
so an agent can recognise its own. The craft in between — judgement, routing, index and log
discipline, validation, deleting processed notes — is genuinely common, and stays stated once.

The tell for this class: a skill that describes the environment ("you are attached", "your
work is a worktree") rather than the work. Environment claims are exactly the part a spawner
varies, so they belong in the briefing, or in a list that covers every case the spawner can
produce.
